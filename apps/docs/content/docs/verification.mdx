## Separate public claims from privileged decisions

A customer-facing endpoint may call <code>submitClaim</code>. A separate server-side route or job may call <code>verifyPayment</code> after authenticating the reviewer and checking their merchant role.

The service should be constructed with a host-owned <code>VerificationAuthorizer</code>:

~~~ts
const verificationAuthorizer: VerificationAuthorizer = {
  authorize: async ({ merchantId, actorId, sourceType, sourceReference }) =>
    accessControl.canVerifyPayments({
      merchantId,
      actorId,
      sourceType,
      sourceReference,
    }),
};

const egyPay = createEgyPay({
  storage,
  verificationAuthorizer,
});
~~~

The authorizer must derive identity from trusted server-side context. Do not accept authorization fields from a customer request body.

## Record provenance

An approval includes explicit authorization metadata:

~~~ts
await egyPay.verifyPayment({
  merchantId,
  paymentIntentId,
  paymentClaimId,
  authorization: {
    actorType: 'HUMAN',
    actorId: authenticatedReviewer.id,
    sourceType: 'merchant_statement',
    sourceReference: checkedRow.id,
    evidenceReference: null,
  },
  note: 'Matched against the merchant statement.',
  idempotencyKey,
});
~~~

The verification record is append-only. Keep source references bounded and avoid logging raw transaction references, wallet numbers, credentials, or evidence contents.

## What trusted means

For human review, “trusted” means the authorized merchant operator actually checked a source they control or otherwise trust. The SDK records the decision and its provenance; it cannot independently attest that the source is genuine.

A future provider adapter must have an official, authorized source and must pass its authenticated identity through the host authorizer. A reconciliation match is a review result, not approval by itself.
